Write A Catalyst and Build it into Existence.

We live in a golden age of productivity tools. Yet, I constantly felt disorganized. Not because I lacked apps — I used all the big ones: Notion, Mem, Obsidian. Each offered promise, structure, and even occasional delight. But something was missing. I still spent too much time filing, searching, linking, maintaining.

Then I found [Fabric.so](https://fabric.so/?via=yogesh). Not by accident, but by need. I wanted something smarter. Something calmer. Something that understood me, not just my notes.

This is my story of how I moved from three well-loved tools to one intelligent, AI-powered workspace — and why you might want to consider doing the same. In last one week i have tried to find out the gaps and used Fabric, here is some finding -

**1\. The Frustration That Sparked My Search**

**Notion** was beautiful, flexible, endlessly customizable. But that was the problem: it demanded structure. My days became database tweaks and page nesting.

**Obsidian** offered raw power and markdown control. Graphs! Plugins! Local files! But everything required intent. If I didn’t link my notes manually, they stayed isolated.

**Mem** was smart. It resurfaced notes, pulled context, and whispered things I’d forgotten. Yet it lived in a closed garden. No PDFs. Limited file types. It felt like a journal, not a vault.

> I needed a tool that met me in the middle. That worked *with* my mind, not against its mess.

**2\. Discovering** [**Fabric.so**](https://fabric.so/?via=yogesh)

Fabric didn’t show up in a flashy ad. I stumbled across it in a Reddit thread titled: “Notion is great, but do you ever feel like you’re working for your notes?”

That hit hard.

Within ten minutes of testing [Fabric.so](https://fabric.so/?via=yogesh):

- I saved a screenshot.
- Dropped a PDF.
- Created a quick note.
- Searched: “When did I write about AI and attention spans?”

Fabric showed me the exact note, highlighted the phrase, and suggested related items I’d forgotten.  
That’s when I knew: this wasn’t just a tool. It was a partner.

**3\. Notion vs.** [**Fabric**](https://fabric.so/?via=yogesh)**: The Builder vs. The Assistant**

Notion is like a blank canvas with a construction kit. You can build anything: wikis, CRMs, blogs, dashboards. But that flexibility comes at a cost. ==You must design your system before it helps you.==

I spent months creating a life OS in Notion. It worked — for a while. But every new project meant more structuring.

Fabric said: “Just drop it in. We’ll handle the rest.”

No properties. No tables. No dashboards. Yet somehow, everything felt more findable. Fabric’s AI tagged my uploads, grouped by themes, and surfaced items I didn’t remember saving.

It wasn’t trying to be a database. It was trying to be a brain.

**4\. Obsidian vs.** [**Fabric**](https://fabric.so/?via=yogesh)**: Manual Mastery vs. Machine Memory**

Obsidian is for the digital craftsman. You write in Markdown, create links, install plugins, build your perfect PKM vault.

It feels empowering — if you love tweaking systems.

I enjoyed it. For a while. But it required constant grooming. Notes disconnected too easily. Backlinks meant nothing if I didn’t manually create them.

Fabric removed the need for maintenance. I didn’t *have* to link. The AI understood related content. When I searched “audience segmentation,” it found an old Evernote I imported, a PDF I saved last week, and a screenshot of a tweet from months ago.

Obsidian showed me what I linked. Fabric showed me what I *forgot*.

**5\. Mem vs.** [**Fabric**](https://fabric.so/?via=yogesh)**: Smart Notes vs. Smart Everything**

Mem felt close to what I wanted. It used AI to resurface notes and understood context. But it was narrow. Mostly text-based.

Fabric is wide.

I can:

- Save a YouTube link.
- Drop a 40MB PDF.
- Screenshot a slide.
- Paste a Twitter thread.

All of it gets auto-tagged, summarized, and indexed.

Mem felt like a smart journal. Fabric feels like a digital world.

**6\. One Week with** [**Fabric**](https://fabric.so/?via=yogesh)**: A Real Diary**

**Day 1**: Captured random ideas via mobile. Scanned handwritten notes. Was shocked Fabric made the images searchable.

**Day 2**: Imported Notion pages and Dropbox folders. Search felt like magic.

**Day 3**: Recorded a meeting. Fabric transcribed and summarized it.

**Day 4**: Created a Space for a blog project. Dropped references, sketches, voice notes. Fabric showed related tweets I saved months ago.

**Day 5**: Got a recap email: “This week, you mostly saved content about UI design and digital minimalism.”

That insight alone was worth the price.

**7\. Collaboration: Google Docs Meets AI Memory**

In Notion, collaboration is structured. You comment on a block. Share a page. Update a database.

In Fabric, it’s fluid.

- Share a Space.
- Everyone drops content.
- Fabric ties it together.
- The AI assistant answers questions from anyone: “What’s our current launch status?”

It acts like a shared brain for small teams. No admin required.

**8\. Visual Thinking and Media**

I’m a visual person. Notion buried my screenshots in blocks. Obsidian didn’t preview images. Mem showed thumbnails, but limited file support.  
[**Fabric**](https://fabric.so/?via=yogesh) shines here:

- Grid views.
- Image previews.
- Annotated PDFs.
- Searchable screenshots.

It turned my chaotic camera roll into a knowledge base.

**9\. Search That Feels Like Magic**

Every app claims to have good search. Fabric actually does.  
I can type:

- “Find the deck with the red slide about retention.”
- “Show everything related to focus and dopamine.”
- “That image I took of the Kanban board at work.”

And Fabric shows me. Sometimes before I finish typing.

**10\. AI That Earns Trust**

I’m skeptical of AI summaries. But Fabric includes references. When I ask it to summarize a report, it shows me where the insights came from.  
When I ask “What did I learn this month?” it offers a thoughtful, accurate recap.  
It doesn’t just regurgitate. It reflects.

**11\. Where** [**Fabric**](https://fabric.so/?via=yogesh) **Still Needs Work**

No tool is perfect.

- It lacks full Markdown export.
- Power users may miss Obsidian-style plugins.
- Some features (like syncing large drives) are Pro-only.

But the team is fast-moving. They ship weekly. And the core experience already outpaces the alternatives.

- Free: 250MB, 100 AI credits
- Basic: $5/month, 50GB, voice transcription
- Pro: $12.50/month, 2TB, integrations, power features

No lock-in. Cancel anytime. (Price could be different by time visit- [**Fabric**](https://fabric.so/?via=yogesh)**.so**)  
Compared to the hours saved? It’s a bargain.

**13\. Why** [**Fabric**](https://fabric.so/?via=yogesh) **Became My Default Tool**

It respects my time. It handles all formats. It connects what I didn’t think to connect. It reflects what I’ve been doing. It feels like a memory layer, not a filing cabinet.

Most tools store. [**Fabric**](https://fabric.so/?via=yogesh) remembers.  
Most tools let you write. [**Fabric**](https://fabric.so/?via=yogesh) helps you think.  
Most tools react. [**Fabric**](https://fabric.so/?via=yogesh) anticipates.

**14\. Final Verdict: One Tool to Rule My Mind**

Notion is still great. Obsidian is still powerful. Mem is still clever.  
But [**Fabric**](https://fabric.so/?via=yogesh) feels inevitable.  
Like what happens when AI is deeply woven into your workflow, not bolted on. Like what happens when tools adapt to people, not vice versa.  
If you’re overwhelmed by information, drowning in folders, or tired of note systems that require maintenance — try [**Fabric**](https://fabric.so/?via=yogesh).  
Not because it’s perfect. But because it works like your mind does.  
And once your second brain starts working for you? You might finally have space to use the first one.
